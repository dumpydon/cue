What does Cue actually do?

Cue is a desktop AI copilot. It can use the conversation you capture, your selected screen context, and your saved meeting mode to suggest replies, explain concepts, help with code, or recap a session. It works with the model provider you choose.

Is Cue free?

The app is free and open source. You bring your own provider credentials, and those providers may charge for model or transcription usage. Local Whisper transcription is available when its runtime and a model are installed; cloud chat requests still go to your chosen provider.

Will Cue show up in my screen share?

Cue starts in incognito and requests native screen-capture protection. This is best effort on macOS: capture behavior varies, and some ScreenCaptureKit-based tools can include protected windows. Test your exact sharing setup before relying on it. The comparison above is an illustration, not a universal guarantee.

Does it record both sides of a call?

With your permission, Cue can transcribe your microphone and system audio as separate channels. Meeting audio is an opt-in setting on macOS. macOS shows a recording or sharing indicator when system capture is active; incognito does not remove OS indicators.

Where does my data go?

Settings, keys, meeting contexts, files, and saved sessions live locally. Requests send the needed context to the provider you select; cloud transcription sends audio to the selected speech provider. Local transcription keeps that audio processing on your Mac. This website does not record your screen or microphone. Read the privacy guide.

Which Macs can run Cue?

The complete meeting-audio experience targets macOS 14.4 or later. Choose the download matching your Mac’s processor. This is a developer preview, locally signed and not Apple-notarized; the download guide explains what that means before you install.

Can I use my own models and meeting context?

Yes. Save separate Fast and Smart model choices, add custom model IDs, or use a compatible custom endpoint. Modes let you edit a title and instructions, reorder them, and add reference files. Switching modes brings that context into later requests.
