Your data, your choices

Privacy,
in plain language.

Cue runs on your desktop. You choose the providers, the context, and when to capture.

This website.

This site does not access your microphone, capture your screen, or run the Cue desktop app. Product examples contain synthetic content. No analytics scripts, advertising pixels, or tracking cookies are included. Cloudflare serves normal website requests; downloads use GitHub’s release hosting.

What stays on your Mac.

Settings, provider keys, model choices, meeting modes, extracted reference-file text, and saved sessions live in local application storage. Saved sessions can contain transcript turns, answers, and notes. Local storage is not a promise of encryption at rest.

What goes to a provider.

When you ask for help, Cue sends relevant context to the provider or compatible endpoint you select. Depending on the action and settings, this can include your question, recent conversation, selected mode instructions, reference-file text, profile context, and a screenshot. Your provider’s terms, retention rules, and pricing apply.

Speech and recording.

Cloud transcription sends audio to the selected speech provider. Local Whisper processes speech on your Mac after a model is downloaded. Local transcription does not make cloud chat local: a cloud chat provider still receives the context needed to answer.

Microphone and screen or system-audio capture require macOS permission. Meeting audio is opt-in. macOS can show a recording or sharing indicator during capture; incognito does not remove that indicator.

Incognito is best effort.

Cue starts in incognito on each fresh launch and requests native screen-capture protection. It cannot guarantee exclusion from every capture tool. Some ScreenCaptureKit-based apps can include protected windows. Test your actual sharing setup before relying on it.

Your choices.

Stop a session, control meeting audio, change your provider, and choose which mode and reference context to include. Follow the consent requirements and rules that apply to your call.

Ask a question on GitHub ↗. Keep API keys, passwords, private transcripts, and sensitive screenshots out of public reports.
